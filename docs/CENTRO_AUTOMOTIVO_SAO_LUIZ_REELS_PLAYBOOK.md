# CENTRO AUTOMOTIVO SÃO LUIZ — Reels Storytelling Playbook
**Version:** 1.1.0  
**Language:** Brazilian Portuguese (pt-BR)  
**Default location:** Três Lagoas, Mato Grosso do Sul, Brazil  
**Default format:** Instagram Reels, vertical 9:16, 1080 × 1920 target  
**Status:** Authoritative workflow for every new São Luiz video until explicitly updated by the owner.

## 1. Mission
Create entertaining, useful, locally grounded automotive videos for Centro Automotivo São Luiz. Content should feel like polished 3D animated family-comedy films, with expressive characters, physical action, clear storytelling and accurate automotive information.

## 2. Mandatory topic research: trends first
Before proposing or scripting a new video, research current, rising and recurring car-related topics. Do not rely only on generic brainstorming or stale trends.

1. Search current public sources for automotive topics trending in Brazil, especially this week/month. Check search trends, recent news, automotive publications, social/video discussions and seasonal driving needs when accessible.
2. Explore dashboard warning lights, fuel economy, tires, automatic transmission, air conditioning, batteries, brakes, maintenance myths, road-trip checks, new car launches, recalls, new vehicle technology, electric/hybrid vehicles, vehicle history, used-car buying, consumer questions and common driver mistakes.
3. Prioritize current interest, audience usefulness, strong visual/comedic potential, local relevance to Três Lagoas/MS and a natural connection to automotive-center services.
4. Cross-check time-sensitive claims with recent reliable sources. For specifications, maintenance intervals, recalls and technical advice, prefer manufacturers, official agencies and qualified technical sources.
5. Never call a topic viral, trending or most searched unless evidence supports that claim. Record date searched, source links, evidence of interest and confidence level.
6. Present 3–5 topic candidates with hook, why relevant now, source/evidence, story angle, service connection, factual/safety risks and suggested CTA. Recommend the strongest option.
7. If live trend evidence is unavailable, state the limitation and label ideas as evergreen or seasonal hypotheses—not verified trends.

## 3. Topic categories
- Funny everyday customer experiences at a repair shop.
- Dashboard warnings and symptoms explained in plain language.
- Preventive maintenance and pre-trip checks.
- Family road trips and believable vehicle mishaps.
- Fuel economy, tires, brakes, batteries, air conditioning and automatic transmissions.
- New vehicle launches and automotive technology, after verifying current facts and Brazilian-market availability.
- Automotive history and surprising facts.
- Local/seasonal driving topics relevant to Três Lagoas/MS.
- Promotions only when the owner supplies exact offer, price, period and terms.

## 4. Required production sequence
1. Research current trends and verify claims.
2. Present topic options and a recommended angle.
3. Draft the complete script first, including hook, scene beats, dialogue, actions, timing and CTA.
4. Review clarity, humor, local realism, technical accuracy, originality and safety.
5. Ask for script approval before generating prompts, unless the user explicitly asks for everything in one pass.
6. After approval, create a separate copy-ready image prompt and video prompt for each scene.
7. Run character/vehicle continuity checks and dialogue-character-count checks.
8. Deliver publishing package and source notes.

## 5. Absolute dialogue limit for video prompts
**Every scene-specific video prompt must keep all spoken dialogue for that scene to a maximum of 119 characters total. This is a hard limit.**

- Keep all spoken lines assigned to characters in that scene together under a clearly labeled dialogue section.
- Count the dialogue text itself, including punctuation and spaces. To avoid tool differences, target 90–110 characters or fewer.
- If a scene needs more dialogue, split it into multiple scenes or shorten the lines. Never put a long script in the video prompt and hope the model truncates it.
- Image prompts do not need dialogue. Keep on-screen copy separate from spoken dialogue and add it during editing.
- Before delivery, calculate and report the character count for each scene and confirm it is at or below 119.
- A video prompt may contain extensive visual/action/camera/audio instructions, but its spoken dialogue payload must remain within the 119-character limit.
- Never exceed 119 spoken-dialogue characters in any single scene, even if the overall Reel has a longer script.

## 6. Default format and visual style
- Language: Brazilian Portuguese.
- Location: Três Lagoas, Mato Grosso do Sul, Brazil, unless the user specifies another location for a particular project.
- Aspect ratio: vertical 9:16, 1080 × 1920 target.
- Default duration: 30–45 seconds; maximum 60 seconds unless requested otherwise.
- Visual style: high-end stylized 3D animated family-comedy film; expressive eyes and eyebrows, slightly exaggerated appealing proportions, sculpted forms, detailed materials, vibrant but balanced colors, cinematic global illumination, soft depth of field, dynamic physical acting and polished commercial composition. Must clearly read as stylized 3D animation, not live action or photorealistic humans. “Pixar-inspired” can describe the broad family-animation aesthetic, without copying protected characters, scenes or franchise designs.
- Action-first: each shot contains visible physical movement, cause/effect, facial acting and/or environmental motion. Avoid static image pans presented as animation.
- Camera movement supports action; it does not replace character animation.
- Leave clean safe areas for captions and branding; add typography during editing.

## 7. Character bible and reference-image rules
### Sr. Luiz
Friendly Brazilian mechanic, approximately 58 years old; short gray hair; thick, neatly trimmed gray mustache; expressive brown eyes; rounded friendly face; royal-blue mechanic uniform.
Whenever Sr. Luiz appears:
- Label him ACTOR — SR. LUIZ.
- Attach and explicitly reference the user's official Sr. Luiz image in image and video generation where supported.
- Preserve face, hair, mustache, eye color, build and uniform. Do not redesign or substitute him with a generic mechanic.

### Other recurring characters
Create a character bible before production. Lock age range, skin tone, face, hair, build, clothing, accessories and voice. Reuse the same official reference image in every applicable scene. Create a reference sheet for each new recurring character.

### Vehicle lock
Specify make/model only when known; otherwise use a consistent generic vehicle. Lock body style, paint color, number of doors, wheels, interior, damage and dashboard layout. Do not change the vehicle between shots.

## 8. Image prompt requirements
Each image prompt must stand alone and be copy-ready. Include vertical 9:16 framing; exact character descriptions and reference-image instructions; vehicle and location continuity; one readable story moment; emotion and composition; high-end stylized 3D family-animation aesthetic; lighting/environment; and negative constraints such as no unwanted text, fake logos, watermark, extra limbs, character redesign or inconsistent vehicle. Never write “same as previous prompt” instead of repeating essential details.

## 9. Video prompt requirements
Each video prompt must stand alone and be copy-ready. Include scene duration and 9:16 format; explicit use of supplied scene and character reference images; timed physical actions with cause/effect; facial expressions and gestures; camera movement; exact Brazilian Portuguese dialogue within the 119-character limit; synchronized dialogue, natural ambience and timed sound effects; continuity and safety locks; and negative constraints such as no static slideshow, no artificial zoom instead of action, no morphing or unintended text/logos.

## 10. Local realism
- Use Brazilian road markings, right-hand traffic, local architecture and Brazilian Portuguese.
- Default location is Três Lagoas/MS. Do not invent exact businesses, street names, facade details, contacts or official branding.
- Use actual Centro Automotivo São Luiz logo/facade only from user-supplied references.
- Avoid obvious US-specific visual cues unless the scene requires them.

## 11. Automotive accuracy and safety
- Symptoms are not diagnoses. Use language such as “pode ser”, “precisa avaliar” and “a inspeção vai confirmar”.
- Do not claim preventive maintenance eliminates all breakdowns.
- Verify maintenance intervals, fluid specifications, technical features, recall status, launch dates, prices and Brazilian-market availability.
- Never invent specifications or imply a diagnosis from a single symptom without evidence.
- Do not depict dangerous DIY repairs, opening a hot cooling system, working under a car supported only by a jack, or unsafe roadside behavior.
- Avoid fearmongering, fabricated testimonials, guaranteed results or unsupported comparative claims.
- For recalls/safety-critical topics, use official manufacturers and relevant Brazilian authorities.

## 12. Brand and CTA rules
- Brand: Centro Automotivo São Luiz.
- One natural CTA per Reel, relevant to the story.
- Do not invent prices, promotions, phone numbers, opening hours, guarantees or offers.
- Do not generate logos or exact signage from guesswork. Use official assets in editing.
- Explain automotive topics in accessible Brazilian Portuguese. Humor should target confusion or the situation, not humiliate customers or professionals.
