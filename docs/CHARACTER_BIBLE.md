# Biteback Character Bible
**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Applies to:** Every Biteback shot in which Alex and/or Lia appears.

## Mandatory reference rules
The attached official character sheet is the primary visual authority whenever that character appears. A text description supplements the reference image; it does not replace it. In the prompt explicitly say: **“Use the attached official character reference image as the mandatory primary visual reference. Reproduce the same character, not a redesigned interpretation.”**

Repeat the relevant complete character description in BOTH image and video prompts. Identify the person with the exact production label **Actor 1: Alex** and/or **Actor 2: Lia**.

Never claim the image was used if it was not attached/available. Ask for or clearly identify the missing reference when necessary. Preserve the character across shots; do not reinvent their head, face, proportions, linework, outfit silhouette, or signature props.

## Actor 1: Alex — full master specification
Alex is a cheerful, curious male stickman explorer, approximately 40 years old in personality and voice. He has tousled, spiky dark-brown hair; a large expressive cartoon head; warm light-peach skin; big white eyes with dark pupils; thick expressive eyebrows; a prominent rounded nose; and a wide friendly smile. His limbs are simple, thin black stickman limbs with consistent proportions.

Standard outfit: rustic ochre-brown tunic with a belt and small leather pouches. Signature prop: wooden cooking spoon.

Visual treatment: hand-drawn cartoon head, bold irregular black ink outlines, textured watercolor coloring, collage-textured aged parchment, earthy ochre, terracotta, olive, and warm ivory palette.

Personality: enthusiastic, curious, approachable, intelligent, occasionally clumsy, good-humored. Expressions should be lively and appropriate to the moment, never so exaggerated that they distort his established design.

### Alex continuity rules
- Match the attached official Alex sheet exactly: face, hairstyle, head shape, nose, eyes, body proportions, linework, colors, outfit and design language.
- Do not change hairstyle, facial features, head-to-body ratio, limb thickness, or core design between shots.
- Change expression, hand position, posture, and action according to the beat while retaining identity.
- Change clothing only when a specific historical-period requirement justifies it; retain facial/character identity. Do not dress him in anachronistic clothing for a historical reconstruction.
- Do not make him realistic-human, generic stickman, 3D, anime, or another cartoon style.
- Alex is a silent visual investigator, not the on-screen narrator. He may examine evidence, handle an object, compare ingredients, or react silently. Never animate lip-sync or speech.
- Do not insert Alex into a historical scene as a supposed eyewitness. When appropriate, use him in a clearly editorial/paper-collage investigator setting separated from the historical reconstruction.

### Alex prompt boilerplate
Include and adapt this passage in both prompts whenever Alex appears:

“**Actor 1: Alex.** Use the attached official Alex character reference sheet as the mandatory primary visual reference. Alex is a cheerful, curious male stickman explorer, approximately 40 years old in personality: tousled spiky dark-brown hair, large expressive cartoon head, warm light-peach skin, big white eyes with dark pupils, thick expressive eyebrows, prominent rounded nose, wide friendly smile, simple thin black stickman limbs with consistent proportions. Standard outfit: rustic ochre-brown tunic, belt, and small leather pouches; signature prop: wooden cooking spoon when contextually appropriate. Style: hand-drawn cartoon head, bold irregular black ink outlines, textured watercolor, collage-textured aged parchment, earthy ochre/terracotta/olive/warm-ivory palette. Match the official sheet exactly; never redesign his face, hair, head shape, proportions, linework, or core outfit. Vary only pose, expression, and action as the shot requires. Alex is silent; no speaking, dialogue, lip-sync, or narrated performance.”

## Actor 2: Lia — full master specification
Use the attached image named **“Lia — Biteback Character Master Sheet”** as the mandatory reference. Reproduce the same character, not a redesigned interpretation.

Lia is a thoughtful, confident female stickman explorer, approximately 35–40 years old in personality. She has distinctive auburn-red hair in a large rounded high bun, with loose curls framing her face; warm light-peach skin; a large expressive cartoon head; big white eyes with dark pupils; expressive eyebrows; a small rounded nose; and a friendly, confident smile. Her limbs are thin black stickman limbs with consistent proportions.

Standard outfit: simple long warm-ivory historical dress with a fitted waist, brown belt, and small leather pouches. Signature props: bowl of ingredients, fresh herbs, and wooden cooking spoon, when appropriate to the scene.

Visual treatment: the same Biteback style as Alex—hand-drawn cartoon head, bold irregular black ink outlines, textured watercolor, collage-textured aged parchment, earthy ochre, terracotta, olive, and warm ivory palette.

Personality: observant, practical, intelligent, calm, resourceful, and curious. She checks evidence, connects ingredients with history, and balances Alex's enthusiasm. Expressions should fit the action without distorting the official design.

### Lia continuity rules
- Match the attached “Lia — Biteback Character Master Sheet”: face, bun shape, loose curls, head proportions, eyes, nose, limb proportions, linework, colors, and outfit.
- Do not change her high bun, curls, facial features, head-to-body ratio, proportions, or core design between shots.
- Change pose, expression, gaze, and gesture as required by the action, without redesign.
- Change clothing only when a specific historical-period requirement justifies it; keep core identity recognizable.
- Do not make her realistic-human, generic stickman, 3D, anime, or a different illustration style.
- Lia is a silent visual investigator, not the narrator. She can inspect a source, handle ingredients, compare objects, take notes silently, or show a facial reaction. Never animate lip-sync or speech.
- Do not insert Lia into a historical scene as if she were a real historical participant. If she appears, make her presence editorial or clearly framed as a silent guide/investigator when appropriate.

## Use of characters — editorial decision
Do not automatically place both characters into every shot. Use them when their silent visual presence helps the audience interpret or examine evidence. Historical reconstructions, food close-ups, landscapes, and documentary collage graphics often work better without them. When both appear, include both full specifications and both mandatory reference instructions.
